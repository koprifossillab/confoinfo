Field Trip

Walking through the tidal flats, southwestern coasts of Korea

Date 2026.10.30.~11.1. (2 Nights and 3 Days)
Start Gunsan city
End Busan Train Station
Guide Tae Soo Chang, Hun Jun Ha, Serin Lim, U-Seong Kim, So Lin Kim, Heechae Jung

Description

Along the west coast of Korean peninsula, extensive tidal flats have been developed, these having been formed during the mid-Holocene, ca. 8-6 thousands year. However, the tidal flats have different aspects from place to place in response to dominant physical forcing. The field excursion aims at observing sedimentary facies characteristics and stratigraphic evolution during the late Quaternary coupled with sea-level fluctuations. This field trip consists of various types of tidal flats including typical estuarine to wave-dominated open coasts associated with coastal dunes.

Morphodynamics and sedimentation of the macrotidal flat, southern Ganghwa Island

Date 2026.10.30.~11.1. (2 Nights and 3 Days)
Start Incheon International Airport
End Seoul Train Station
Guide Seolhui Bang, Seungyeon Sohn, Kyungsik Choi

Description

This field excursion provides a comprehensive examination of the morphology, morphodynamics, and sedimentation of Korea's most extensive macrotidal flats. Conducted over three days, the trip offers participants a rare opportunity to observe the dynamic sedimentary environments of the Han River Delta, including areas adjacent to the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ). Key topics addressed during the excursion include the origin and significance of inclined heterolithic stratification, the morphodynamics of tidal dunes and channels, the influence of tidal and seasonal processes, and the stratigraphic architecture of macrotidal flats. On-site examination of core peels will facilitate discussion on the coupled roles of tidal and wave processes in governing the morphodynamic evolution of these macrotidal systems.

Jeju Island UNESCO Global Geopark Excursion

Date 2026.11.5.~11.6. OR 2026.10.31~11.1. (1 Nights and 2 Days)
Start Jeju Airport at 11:00 AM
End Jeju Airport at 5:00 PM
Guide Jeon Yongmun

Description

This field trip offers participants the opportunity to visit key geological sites of Jeju Island, designated as both a UNESCO World Natural Heritage site and a UNESCO Global Geopark, to directly experience the geological heritage of the island. Jeju Island is renowned for its unique volcanic landforms and rich maritime culture. The trip aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of both the geological features and the cultural-historical background that have shaped the island.

Coastal Landforms and Wave-Dominated Settings on the Korean Peninsula Central East Coast

Date 2026.11.5.~11.7. (2 Nights and 3 Days)
Start Gangneug Station
End Yeongdeok Station
Guide Kwang Hee Choi

Description

The central east coast of the Korean Peninsula is a microtidal coast with a tidal range of less than 0.5 meters and is relatively rapidly uplifting on the peninsula. Coarse-sand beaches, coastal dunes, and lagoons are well developed. Additionally, along the rocky coast, sea cliffs, shore platforms, and sea stacks appear. These coastal features are totally different from those of the west coast, which has a highly complex coastline and a wide intertidal zone.

In the Gangneung area, we will encounter sand beaches, lagoons, and coastal terraces (Figure a). In particular, the Jeongdongjin coastal terrace in Gangneung developed due to the combined effects of sea-level fluctuations and tectonic uplift caused by fault movement, resulting in a horizontal shore platform and beach ridge characteristic of wave-dominated coasts. Traces of fossilized boring shells from the past also remain on the terrace surface (Figure b).

In the Uljin and Yeongdong areas, we observe coastal plains formed by beach ridge advancement and note the southward shift of erosion zones caused by recent coastal structure installations (Figures c and d).
